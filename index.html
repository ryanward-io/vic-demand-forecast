    Backtest: how each model would have done

    Every day in the window, each model stands at midnight with only the data known by then and forecasts the next 48 hours. After midnight it sees the weather forecast that existed at the time (archived by Open-Meteo), never the weather that actually happened, and models that learn were trained only on data from before the window. MAE is the average miss in megawatts; MAPE the same as a percentage. Lower is better.

    The models

    Same as yesterday · Same day last week · Average of last 4 weeks
    Benchmarks. Anything cleverer has to beat these to be worth running.
    MSTL + ETS
    Classical statistics: split the last 8 weeks into a trend plus daily and weekly cycles, then extend the trend with exponential smoothing. Demand only, no weather.
    LightGBM (calendar + weather)
    Gradient-boosted decision trees, the workhorse of forecasting competitions. Learns from 2019 onwards how demand on a given half-hour departs from its recent average, given the weather forecast, solar radiation, day of week and public holidays.
    LSTM (seq2seq)
    A small recurrent neural network trained from scratch: one LSTM reads the last week, a second walks through the next two days using the weather forecast and calendar.
    Chronos-2 (foundation model)
    Amazon's pretrained time-series transformer (2025). It has never been trained on this data: it reads the last 12 weeks of demand with weather and holidays and forecasts "zero-shot", the way a language model answers without fine-tuning.
    Average of LightGBM and Chronos-2
    A simple ensemble: the two make different kinds of mistakes, so their average is often better than either.
